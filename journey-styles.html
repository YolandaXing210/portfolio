Selected work / VR installation

Journey to
the Forgotten

A body-centered VR journey

Timeframe
March–June 2024
Type
Solo project
Focus
Embodied interaction, worldbuilding, HCI
Outcome
VR game
Tools
Unity, Meta Quest

A VR interactive experience that explores embodiment in virtual space through spatial layout, bodily movement, and interaction design. By staging resistance, ritualized motion, and attention-driven environments, the work shifts VR away from mind-centric control toward physical awareness. Its goal is to reframe immersion as an embodied process of participation and acceptance rather than navigation or achievement.

01 / Start with

From abstract control to bodily awareness.

Inspiration

Inspired by Francis Alÿs’ performance Sheep (2002), where collective movement reads like a choreography of the mind: drifting attention, social gravity, and quiet surrender.

In VR, participants are often treated as primarily “attention-centric” viewers, present through attention but detached from physical consequence.

How can a virtual world guide players from abstract control to bodily awareness through interaction alone?

Rather than telling a story through narrative, this project treats the body itself as the narrative medium.

Problem: interaction as a design material

Many VR experiences mistake immersion for participation, leaving users visually present but agency-less: disembodied observers. That exposes ethical flaws in interaction design.

  1. Immersion ≠ participation
  2. Voyeuristic immersion
  3. Disembodied presence
  4. Loss of participant subjectivity
  5. Interaction as ethical positioning

Conclusion

With interaction understood as a material that shapes participant subjectivity, I approached the design process as a gradual reconstruction of the body’s role in virtual space.

Ideation: a spectrum of interaction in VR

Rather than treating interaction as a fixed category, I frame VR interaction as a spectrum of bodily involvement, from visual immersion to embodied participation:

360 videoDisembodied observation
  • Fixed camera position
  • No bodily consequence
  • No agency beyond attention
  • Strong voyeuristic positioning
Abstract inputSymbolic control
  • Relies on buttons, joysticks, and triggers
  • Efficient and familiar
  • Agency without embodiment
  • The body operates the system from outside
Representational interactionPartial body
  • Increased presence
  • Partial embodiment
  • The body functions primarily as a tool
Embodied interactionBody as medium
  • Sustained physical effort
  • Rhythm, posture, and repetition matter
  • Movement carries emotional and expressive meaning

Participants are a point of view.The body itself becomes the interface.
