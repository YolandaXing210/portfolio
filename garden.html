01 / The Question

AI can recognize what we carry. But can it understand why we carry it?

Take a plush rabbit. Computer vision can say “this is a plush rabbit.” To the person carrying it, it might be a gift from their mother, the thing they brought when they moved abroad, a keepsake from a relationship—or just a habit.

Everyday objects hold meanings that are invisible to computer vision: attachment, memory, comfort, habit, loss. This project starts from that gap.

From an exploratory survey of 20 people and two follow-up interviews:

55%use few or none of the items they carry daily
70%carry at least one unexpected item
80%have one item that represents themselves
90%of people in a relationship carry something connected to their partner

People described what they rarely use but keep anyway in terms of comfort, habit, identity, and emotional safety. A function-based label can’t explain why we carry things—which raised the question of what AI should, and shouldn’t, do with them.

02 / The Interaction Model

The person gives the meaning. AI gives it a form.

The AI never tells you what an object means to you. You give the object meaning; AI transforms it and gives it back.

  1. ObjectYou take something out of your bag.
  2. AI recognizes“What is it?” Gemini names the object and reads its two dominant colors and how visually dense it is.
  3. Human reflects“What does it mean to me?” You choose its emotional role—Comfort, Productivity, Expression, or Inner Self—and write it a short message.
  4. AI interpretsThe relationship and the object’s visual qualities become the parameters of a flower.
  5. Form emergesA generative flower grows; your objects gather into a bouquet in your bag.
  6. Object speaks backGemini writes a reply in the object’s voice, answering your message. It’s printed on a receipt.

What the human contributes

  • Memory
  • Emotion
  • Meaning

What AI contributes

  • Recognition
  • Transformation
  • Generative response

To keep AI from taking over meaning-making, the system does not:

03 / The Experience

Physical object → digital interaction → generative form → physical artifact.

The experience is designed to be slow: short pauses leave room for anticipation and reflection.

04 / From Emotion to Form

Visualizing the relationship, not the object.

Instead of visualizing the objects, I wanted to visualize the invisible relationships around them. A flower can vary emotionally without ranking anything, so each object becomes a flower that expresses its relationship, not its function.

The AI does not draw the flowers. Each one is built procedurally in Three.js: the emotional role the person chooses sets the kind of flower, and the visual qualities Gemini reads from the object set its color and fullness.

Emotional role Flower Form & motion e.g.
Comfort Lotus-like Curved, layered petals; gentle sway medicine, lip balm, hand cream, snacks
Productivity Tulip Upright petals; minimal movement laptop, charger, notebook, headphones
Expression Wavy flower Recursive 3D branching; flows in many directions keychains, stickers, accessories
Inner Self Baby’s breath Delicate L-system clusters; soft bobbing handwritten notes, old receipts, photos, charms
Primary color

the object’s dominant color, read by Gemini

Secondary color

its accent color

Density (1–10)

how full the object looks, which sets the number of petals or branches

Flowers gather into a bouquet inside a photo of the person’s own bag—different and unranked, the way objects coexist in a bag. Bouquets from many people can form a shared garden.

05 / The Receipt

A note from your object.

The experience ends not with an AI response on a screen, but with a physical artifact the participant takes away.

It lists every object and its emotional role, then each object’s reply—written by Gemini in the object’s own voice.

You, to a water bottle

“You keep me going through long days.”

The water bottle

“I’m here for you, always ready. Your hydration is my purpose.”

Printing on a 58mm thermal printer from macOS meant bypassing the default print pipeline and sending raw ESC/POS commands from a small Python server. That workaround is documented in my GitHub repository.

06 / Reflection

Recognition is not understanding.

Gemini could name a lip balm, a mouse, a pair of sunglasses. It couldn’t know which were there for comfort, or for someone. That part had to come from the person.

The most meaningful role for AI was not to define the user’s relationship with an object, but to give that relationship a new form.

Designing with AI, building with AI

I built the project with AI too, through vibe coding—with the same division of roles. When design questions came up mid-build, the AI wrote a plan, I validated it, and only then did it implement.

My roleAI’s role
Define structureExecute logic
Validate plansGenerate code
Provide equationsHandle syntax
Detect driftIterate fast
JudgeApply

Watching friends respond emotionally convinced me that softness, structure, and technical authorship can coexist—an informal check, not a study.
