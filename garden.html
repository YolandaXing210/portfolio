01 / The Question

AI can recognize what we carry. But can it understand why we carry it?

Take a plush rabbit. Computer vision can say “this is a plush rabbit.” To the person carrying it, it might be a gift from their mother, the thing they brought when they moved abroad, a source of safety, a keepsake from a relationship—or nothing at all, just a habit.

Everyday objects often hold meanings that are invisible to computer vision: attachment, memory, comfort, habit, loss. This project starts from that gap.

An exploratory survey of 20 people about what they carry, followed by two interviews, showed the gap between use and meaning:

55%

use only a few, or almost none, of the items they carry every day

70%

carry at least one uncommon or unexpected item

80%

identified one item that represents themselves

90%

of people in a relationship carry at least one item connected to their partner

Interviewees described the things they rarely use but keep anyway in terms of comfort, habit, identity, and emotional safety. A function-based label can’t explain why these objects are carried. That pointed the project toward an emotion-based way of reading belongings—and toward a question about what AI should, and shouldn’t, do with them.

02 / The Interaction Model

The person gives the meaning. AI gives it a form.

The AI never tells you what an object means to you. The experience runs in a loop: you give the object meaning, AI transforms it, and gives it back to you.

  1. ObjectYou take something out of your bag.
  2. AI recognizes“What is it?” Gemini names the object and reads its two dominant colors and how visually dense it is.
  3. Human reflects“What does it mean to me?” You choose its emotional role—Comfort, Productivity, Expression, or Inner Self—and write it a short message.
  4. AI interpretsThe relationship and the object’s visual qualities become the parameters of a flower.
  5. Form emergesA generative flower grows; your objects gather into a bouquet in your bag.
  6. Object speaks backGemini writes a reply in the object’s voice, answering your message. It’s printed on a receipt.

What the human contributes

  • Memory
  • Emotion
  • Meaning

What AI contributes

  • Recognition
  • Transformation
  • Generative response

To keep AI from taking over the act of meaning-making, I set four rules for the system. It does not:

01

judge objects

02

optimize behavior

03

explain meaning

04

assign correctness

03 / The Experience

Physical object → digital interaction → generative form → physical artifact.

The experience is designed to be slow. Short pauses are built in to leave room for anticipation and reflection.

04 / From Emotion to Form

Visualizing the relationship, not the object.

Instead of visualizing the objects themselves, I wanted to visualize the invisible relationships around them. A flower can vary emotionally without ranking anything, so each object becomes a flower that expresses its relationship rather than its function.

The AI does not draw the flowers. Each flower is built procedurally in Three.js from two kinds of input: the emotional role the person chooses sets the kind of flower, and the visual qualities Gemini reads from the object set its color and fullness.

Comfort

Lotus-like flower

Curved, layered petals with a gentle sway—for what makes us feel anchored or safe.

e.g. medicine, lip balm, hand cream, snacks

Productivity

Tulip

Upright petals with minimal movement—for what helps us stay focused and in control.

e.g. laptop, charger, notebook, headphones

Expression

Wavy flower

Recursive 3D branching that flows in many directions—for what shows identity, taste, or mood.

e.g. keychains, stickers, accessories

Inner Self

Baby’s breath

Delicate, L-system-inspired clusters that bob softly—for what we keep private or sacred.

e.g. handwritten notes, old receipts, photos, charms

Primary color

the object’s dominant color, read by Gemini

Secondary color

its accent color

Density (1–10)

how full the object looks, which sets the number of petals or branches

Flowers gather into a bouquet inside a photo of the person’s own bag—different and unranked, the way objects coexist inside a bag. Bouquets from many participants can form a shared garden.

05 / The Receipt

A note from your object.

The experience ends not with an AI response on a screen, but with a physical artifact the participant can take away.

The receipt lists every object and its emotional role, then each object’s reply—written by Gemini in the object’s own voice, in answer to the person’s message.

You, to a water bottle

“You keep me going through long days.”

The water bottle

“I’m here for you, always ready. Your hydration is my purpose.”

Printing on a 58mm thermal printer from macOS meant bypassing the default print pipeline and sending raw ESC/POS commands from a small Python server. That workaround is documented in my GitHub repository.

06 / Reflection

Recognition is not understanding.

Gemini could name a lip balm, a mouse, a pair of sunglasses. It could not know which of them were there for comfort, or for someone. That part had to come from the person.

The most meaningful role for AI was not to define the user’s relationship with an object, but to give that relationship a new form.

Designing with AI, building with AI

I also built the project with AI, through vibe coding. The same division of roles applied: I set the structure and judged the results; the AI generated and iterated. When design questions came up mid-build, I had the AI write a plan, validated it, and only then let it implement.

My role

Define structure · Validate plans · Provide equations · Detect drift · Judge

AI’s role

Execute logic · Generate code · Handle syntax · Iterate fast · Apply

Watching friends try it and respond emotionally convinced me that softness, structure, and technical authorship can coexist. That was an informal check, not a study.

Next

Expanding the emotional input beyond four roles, blooming the flowers in space with AR, building the collective garden, and following how people’s relationships with their objects change over time.
